Does the extra electron move? No constraint, free boundaries
Correcting an earlier claim. The solver does not weld domains to their kernels. Proximity labelling is only the initial condition; thereafter a free-boundary kernel evolves W at every boundary cell from the density balance (myRho − otherRho)/(myRho + otherRho), and flips ownership when W goes negative. Partitions rearrange. Electron transfer is therefore representable, and the null results of the constrained scans were artefacts of constraining.
The construction. Five H atoms; the leftmost is H−, carrying a second electron domain given a small initZeff so it starts diffuse — the outer, weakly bound electron of a real hydride (affinity 0.75 eV), rather than a hemisphere buried at the same radius as the tight one. A diffuse outer domain has its boundary far from the proton, exactly where a neighbour can compete for cells.
The test. Nothing is held and nothing is scanned. Relax, and watch where the extra charge goes: the panel reports how much of it sits on each site. Spreading along the chain is transport; staying put is localisation.
