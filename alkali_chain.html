A chain of big atoms, one valence electron each
n pseudopotential cores in a line, each a closed shell of net charge +1, each carrying one loosely bound valence electron. A uniform field along the chain drives transport.
Why this and not H− + H. The outer electron of a hydride sees a neutral atom and is held by 0.75 eV of correlation, which non-overlapping domains do not represent — so it lowers its energy by spreading, without limit, and no partition constraint can stop it (RealQM partitions all space; removing space from the partition only hands it to whichever domain the default label points at). A valence electron on an alkali core sees +1 and sits in a real Coulomb well. The pathology is absent by construction rather than suppressed by a constraint.
The core is a pseudopotential. rc is the cutoff radius: inside it the wavefunction is held at zero and the core electrons are not represented at all, so lithium costs one electron rather than three and sodium one rather than eleven. Larger rc is a bigger, softer atom whose valence electron is more diffuse — which is the physical reason alkali metals conduct, and the parameter to scan.
One site is empty. n cores with n electrons is half filling, and single occupancy forbids conduction there outright — every domain is occupied, so nothing has anywhere to go. That is the structural insulator the ring demonstrates, not a transport calculation. So this runs n cores and n−1 electrons: one vacancy, which is what the carrier moves into. Net charge +1.
Why n = 5 by default. Five cores and four electrons keeps NELEC at 4, which puts the solver on the direct per-electron Poisson path (used at 5 electrons or fewer) rather than the total-Poisson + SIC path. That matters: the SIC path fails to converge on the kink chain, oscillating over 0.05 Ha, while the direct path is the one checked against V_ee = 1/R exactly.
What to look for, in order. (1) With field=0, run twice with different dy. If the two energies differ, the result is seed-dependent and nothing further is measurable — that is what killed the ring, the kink and the H− pair. (2) Only if they agree, turn the field on and watch whether the vacancy moves along the chain.
Caveat on the energy. The field enters the wavefunction equation but is not included in the reported energy, so E is the zero-field energy of a field-driven configuration. Differences at fixed field are meaningful; comparing across fields is not.
