The hop in two dimensions
A square lattice of pseudopotential cores, one valence electron each, and one excess electron carried as a domain of its own with no kernel. Its position is stated, not searched for, and the two positions that matter are fixed by symmetry.
Why two dimensions changes the answer. On a chain the carrier has no way round: to advance one site it must pass through an ion, and the on-site configuration is the saddle. That barrier came out at 818 meV. A lattice offers a route between atoms — from the centre of one square, through the midpoint of a bond, to the centre of the next, flanked by two atoms at half a spacing each and never at zero distance from any. An earlier scan of this framework found the difference to be a factor of 5.8: 31.5 eV through an ion against 5.46 eV between two.
Both endpoints are symmetry-fixed, which is what makes a specified partition legitimate here. The carrier at a square centre is invariant under the four-fold symmetry of that site; at a bond midpoint, under the two-fold symmetry of the bond. Each is therefore a stationary point of the energy over partitions, at which the interface condition holds without being imposed — the same footing as the H2 midplane. Neither position is chosen.
What decides it. barrier = E(bond midpoint) − E(square centre). Real hopping semiconductors run 10–500 meV. The chain gave 818, which placed this framework outside that class; if two dimensions brings it under ~500, the class is back within reach.
