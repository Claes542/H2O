H− + H : the smallest transfer there is
Two protons, three electrons. The left kernel carries two domains in a 1+1 shell arrangement — a tight inner electron and a diffuse outer one, which is what H− actually is (affinity 0.75 eV, the outer electron barely held). The right kernel carries one. Net charge −1.
How the shell split is made. Two domains on the same kernel with different initZeff: the tight trial density wins the inner cells, the diffuse one wins the outer shell, so the radial split emerges from the initialisation rather than being imposed. No new machinery, and every domain still carries exactly one unit of charge.
What is being watched. Free boundaries are on, so cells change hands wherever a neighbouring domain has the higher density — the solver's own mechanism, which does rearrange partitions. With bias = 0 the two kernels are identical and the ground state should share the extra electron. With bias > 0 the right kernel is made slightly more attractive and the outer electron should shift toward it. The question is how little bias it takes.
