In RealQM an electron is a real charge density in ordinary 3D space, and the atom is found by minimising a real energy: the localisation cost of the cloud plus its Coulomb attraction to the nucleus. What you are looking at

The energy being minimised is

E(ψ) = ½ ∫ |∇ψ|² dV − ∫ (ψ² / r) dV ,    with   ∫ ψ² dV = 1 .
The first term is a localisation cost: squeezing the charge into a smaller region sharpens its gradient and costs energy (this is what standard quantum mechanics calls kinetic energy — but here nothing is moving; it is the strain energy of a static cloud). The second term is the ordinary Coulomb attraction of that charge to the proton. Their balance fixes the size of the atom. Minimising by relaxing the density in “imaginary time” gives, with no fitting and no free parameters, E = −0.5 Hartree and a cloud ψ ∝ e−r — the exact hydrogen ground state.

Why this is the point

Everything here lives in ordinary three-dimensional space. There is no probability amplitude to interpret, no collapse, no wave function on a 3N-dimensional configuration space. The electron is the charge cloud you see; the atom is the shape that minimises a real energy. That is the whole idea of RealQM, and hydrogen is where you can watch it work and verify the answer yourself.

Honest note: hydrogen is the easy case

Standard quantum mechanics also gets hydrogen exactly — so H alone does not distinguish the two. Where RealQM earns its keep is where the standard picture struggles or turns abstract:

  • Helium. With no electron self-repulsion and two clouds that do not overlap, RealQM gives a ground-state energy near −2.90 Ha — below Hartree–Fock (−2.86) and close to the exact −2.9037. It captures electron correlation from geometry alone, with no configuration-space machinery.
  • The H₂ bond, diamagnetism, spin, the periodic table — all built from the same real charge in 3D. Runnable demonstrations and the full account are in the RealQM Gallery.
