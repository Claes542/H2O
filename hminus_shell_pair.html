H− as a genuine nested 1+1 shell, with an H beside it
Built the way the author specifies: a modified Li with kernel charge +1 and one unit of electron charge in each of two nested shells — inner r < R, outer r ≥ R — combined with an ordinary H atom at distance d.
Why this and not the previous attempt. molecule.js has only angular splits, so the earlier "localised" case was two adjacent blobs, not a nested shell: nothing stopped the carrier migrating onto the nucleus, and it collapsed to −1.43 in two runs of three. A nested shell has the restoring force the author identified — squeeze the inner domain and its density at the interface rises while the outer's falls, pushing the boundary back out — so it should hold. The radial machinery lives in molecule_nucleus.js (USER_SHELL_RADIUS), which is what this page uses.
How the two centres coexist. The shell zones are radial about the box centre, where the H− sits, and in the outer zone each cell goes to the closest listed outer domain. So the outer shell of H− and the H atom's own electron are both listed outer, and the region beyond R divides between them by proximity — a nested H− at the centre and an ordinary H to the side.
What to watch for. If the nested H− develops into H2 surrounded by the electron — the inner and outer shells rearranging so the extra charge wraps both nuclei — then the localised carrier is not merely comparable to the delocalised one, it relaxes spontaneously into it. That is no barrier at all, and the strongest statement available for transport.
