H− in a chain of H atoms
A chain of hydrogen atoms with one site carrying two electron domains, split by a plane as RealQM already treats helium. That site is H−.
Why this is the right construction. Every domain still carries exactly one unit of charge, so nothing in the framework is relaxed — unlike a ghost carrier squeezing between atoms, or a domain holding −2. The chain is N protons and N+1 unit domains, all legitimate. And H− is a real species: electron affinity 0.75 eV, so the extra electron is bound, but barely.
The question. The H− site is degenerate with every other site by symmetry, so the endpoints cost the same. What does the path between them cost? A carrier bound by only 0.75 eV has little holding it in place, so the barrier should be of that order — not the several eV a ghost carrier in the interstitial region gave.
