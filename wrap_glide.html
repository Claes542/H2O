A wrap over two atoms, and whether a bias moves it
A chain of H atoms with one extra electron as a wrap enclosing the middle two. A bias on the far kernel then asks whether the wrap shifts along the chain.
How the wrap is made. Not by seeding a diffuse domain and hoping — that failed, because a diffuse trial carries a small prefactor and wins only the far exterior. Here the shell machinery builds it: the two atoms to be wrapped are listed inner (r < R about the box centre), and the wrap electron together with the remaining chain atoms are listed outer, so the region beyond R divides by proximity. The wrap gets a shell around the middle pair; the outer atoms keep their own territory.
Why this configuration should hold. The wrap lies outside every nucleus and competes with none. Across two days that is the arrangement that stayed stable and physical (−1.223 to −1.228 over three runs), while every carrier placed near a nucleus either collapsed or needed its interface locked. And the wrap moves by its boundary flowing — taking in the next atom at the front, releasing one at the back — not by a domain translating through occupied space, which is the mode this solver actually has.
