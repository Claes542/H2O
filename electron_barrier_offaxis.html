Passing an ion: on-axis against off-axis
The axial scan gave the carrier a large interstitial preference — on-site double occupancy costs 1.16 Ha (31.5 eV), the Hubbard U — with a channel flat to half of kBT between sites. But a straight path drives the carrier through the next ion, which is not what a carrier in three dimensions would do.
Here the carrier moves from the interstitial region between sites 1 and 2 to the one between sites 2 and 3, detouring perpendicular to the chain by an offset y. Each offset is scanned along the path and the maximum energy recorded, so the barrier is reported as a function of how far the carrier steps aside.
Reading it. Barrier falling steeply with y and approaching kBT (0.00095 Ha) → the carrier goes round the ion at little cost and the framework conducts. Barrier staying of order an eV at every offset → the ion blocks the channel however one goes about it, and transport is activated at best.
