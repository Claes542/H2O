The double slit: what RealQM cannot do

Read the finding first, because it is the only part of this page that is RealQM. The framework as formulated cannot produce a double-slit pattern at all, for three independent reasons: its densities are real and non-negative, and interference needs cancellation; each domain carries one indivisible unit of charge, so it cannot pass through both slits; and in the static formulation electrons relax rather than move, so there is no equation of motion to propagate anything. The second of those is the same postulate that gives charge quantisation and that the conduction work found forbids a metal — one rule, three consequences.
That verdict applies to the real, static formulation and not to the whole programme. The complex time-dependent branch, where a real density is carried by a complex current, has an amplitude that can cancel. Whether a free-boundary domain interferes there is untested, and it is the only place the question could be settled inside RealQM.
Everything below is standard quantum mechanics — the linear Schrödinger equation and de Broglie's guidance law, which together are de Broglie–Bohm mechanics (1927/1952). It is shown because it is the arrangement that survives the three objections above, and because RealQM's ontology fits it unusually well. It is an illustration of an existing interpretation, not a result of this framework.
Two objects, not one. An indivisible lump with a sharp boundary — what a RealQM domain is — and a complex guiding field. The lump passes through one slit and makes one dot. The field passes through both, interferes, and steers where the dots land. Two things are worth keeping apart: the interference is present on every single run, since it is in the field — what needs many electrons is only the visible pattern of dots, which samples it.

Why not simply let the density split and interfere

Two reasons, and they are independent. RealQM's densities are real and non-negative, and interference requires cancellation — two lobes give ψ12+ψ22, no cross term, no fringes. And a sharp free boundary carrying one indivisible unit of charge cannot break in two without violating the postulate that gives charge quantisation.
The deeper obstacle is the one that settled this historically. Schrödinger proposed ψ as a literal charge density in 1926 and gave it up: if one electron is a spread density, it arrives smeared over every bright fringe and deposits a fraction of its charge in each. Experiment gives one dot per electron, the pattern accumulating over thousands (Merli–Missiroli–Pozzi 1976, Tonomura 1989). This page is the arrangement that keeps the realist lump and still gets the fringes.

Is this one electron, many times?

Yes, and exactly so. There is no particle–particle term anywhere: the field does not depend on the lumps, and each lump is moved by the field alone. Running 4000 concurrently is mathematically identical to running one electron 4000 times — they are batched only for speed. That is the case Tonomura ran, with at most one electron in the apparatus at a time, and it is the one that makes ``the electron interferes with itself'' the standard puzzle.
The answer here is that the electron does not interfere with itself — its field does, and the field is a separate object. Every single electron carries its own guiding field; that field passes both slits and interferes on every run. The lump passes through one. So the interference is already complete for one electron, and what accumulates over thousands is only the sampling of where lumps land. The red trajectory is one such electron: watch it take one slit while the field behind it goes through both.
What this does not explain. The field is identical on every run; the only thing that varies is where the lump starts, drawn from |ψ|2 at t=0. So the Born rule is assumed as an initial condition and then preserved by the dynamics — not derived. The model accounts for the pattern and takes the statistics as given, which is the standing weakness of every pilot-wave account and is not repaired here.

What is computed, and what is assumed

The field is the time-dependent Schrödinger equation solved on a grid (ℏ=m=1, Visscher's staggered scheme, hard-wall slits, damped edges). The lumps follow the de Broglie guidance law v = Im(ψ*∇ψ)/|ψ|2, sampled from |ψ|2 at t=0.
The field is computed independently and the lumps follow it. There is no back-reaction --- the honest version, as chosen. Coupling the lump back into its own guiding field is a research problem, not a simulation setting. The lump's internal structure plays no role here either: only its centre is advected, so this demonstrates the ontology and the statistics, not a RealQM computation of the lump itself.

Which equations are solved

1. The guiding field — the linear time-dependent Schrödinger equation, ℏ=m=1:
i ∂ψ/∂t = Hψ,   H = −½∇2 + V
Split as ψ=R+iI, so ∂R/∂t = HI and ∂I/∂t = −HR, integrated by Visscher's staggered leapfrog (R and I offset by half a step, which conserves |ψ|2):
Rn+1 = Rn + Δt·H In+½
In+3/2 = In+½ − Δt·H Rn+1
with H the 5-point Laplacian at dx=1. V is zero everywhere; the wall is imposed as ψ=0 in its cells (V=∞), which avoids the timestep limit a large finite V would bring.
2. The guidance law — de Broglie's, by explicit Euler on the same Δt:
dx/dt = Im(ψ*∇ψ)/|ψ|2 = (R∇I − I∇R)/(R2+I2)
3. Initial condition — Gaussian packet × eik₀x, lumps drawn from |ψ|2 by rejection. 4. The grey curve — time-integrated positive flux at the screen, ∫max(jx,0)dt with jx = R∂xI − I∂xR, the correct quantum arrival distribution.
440×300 cells, Δt=0.2, k₀=0.8, σx=16, σy=55, wall at x=160–162, slits half-width 5 at y=150±22, screen at x=420, 3200 steps, 4000 lumps, damping sponge at the edges so nothing reflects back.
What is not solved: anything from RealQM. No free boundary, no partition into domains, no Coulomb energy minimisation, no non-overlap. The lump is a point advected by v — its sharp boundary is drawn, not computed. The RealQM content here is interpretive: it says what kind of object the particle is, and none of it enters the mathematics.
The reason is a gap the conduction work already names. RealQM in its static form has no equation of motion for electrons at all — nuclei move classically, electrons relax to a minimum. A genuine RealQM double slit needs a propagating free-boundary domain, which exists only in the complex time-dependent branch of the programme. The same obstruction, met from a different direction.

Two checks worth watching. The dot histogram must converge on the flux-weighted |ψ|2 at the screen (grey curve): the ensemble reproduces standard quantum mechanics exactly, so this model adds a definite trajectory without changing a single prediction. And midline crossings must stay at zero — in a symmetric setup the guidance law forbids a lump from crossing the axis, so a lump that took the upper slit lands in the upper half. That is where the model says something standard quantum mechanics declines to say: which slit, with a definite answer.
