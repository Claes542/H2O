The carrier as a shell, and whether it glides
A chain of H atoms with one extra electron, seeded not on an atom but as a shell surrounding the leftmost two. It is outside every nucleus and competes with none of them.
Why this configuration and not the others. Every localised attempt today was unstable and returned energies below the physical floor (−2.0, −1.67, −1.42): a localised H− puts two domains in competition for one nucleus, and with free interfaces there is no confinement cost to hold them apart, so one collapses onto it. Every delocalised attempt was stable and physical (−1.21, −1.223, at the floor). A shell sits outside all the nuclei and competes with none, so nothing collapses.
Why this transport mode evades the earlier objections. The shell does not translate through space, so nothing has to carve out room — that was the 31 eV and the 5.5 eV. And it is not a change of partition topology: the domain stays one connected region throughout, taking in the next atom at the front and releasing one at the back. Only its boundary moves, which is exactly what the solver's free boundary does, flipping cells on density balance.
What to watch. The shell starts around atoms 0 and 1. If its territory extends to take in atom 2 — and further under a bias — the carrier is gliding, and that is conduction by a mode the framework can actually represent.
