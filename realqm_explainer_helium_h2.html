Two electrons: helium and the H₂ bond

Helium and the hydrogen molecule are the same two-electron problem in RealQM — two real charge clouds in ordinary 3-D space, each feeling the nuclei and the other electron, with no self-repulsion and forbidden to overlap. One charge-2 nucleus gives helium; two protons give a molecule. Below are the actual three-dimensional RealQM solvers, running in your browser — not cartoons. 1   Helium — two clouds, one nucleus running below

The RealQM helium solver (charge-2 kernel, two electrons in non-overlapping half-spaces, no self-repulsion). It relaxes the two real charge densities and reports the ground-state energy — it settles near −2.90 Ha, below Hartree–Fock's −2.86 and close to the exact −2.9037. The correlation is bought by geometry: the two clouds avoid each other.

2   The H₂ bond — the same electrons, two protons running below

The RealQM H₂ dissociation curve, compared with restricted Hartree–Fock. As the protons separate, RealQM's two clouds localize onto separate atoms and the energy goes to two neutral hydrogen atoms — the physically correct dissociation, which restricted HF famously gets wrong. The bond is the well at ≈1.4 a₀; the shared charge between the protons is what holds them together.

One solver, two systems

Both panels above run the same idea: two real charge densities ψ₁², ψ₂² in ordinary space, relaxed downhill in the ordinary energy — localisation cost, attraction to the nuclei, repulsion by the other cloud — with no self-repulsion and non-overlapping supports. Move the nuclei and the same machinery gives a different chemistry: helium's correlated ground state, or hydrogen's covalent bond and its correct breaking into two atoms. There is no wave function on a six-dimensional configuration space, and no probability to interpret --- only charge in three-dimensional space and an energy to minimise.

Why this is the case worth seeing

Hydrogen alone (see the one-electron explainer) does not distinguish RealQM from standard quantum mechanics — both get it exactly. Helium and H₂ do: RealQM reaches below Hartree–Fock on helium and dissociates H₂ correctly, from a single principle applied to real charge in 3-D.
