H2 as a function of the interface surface tension
Two protons at separation R, one electron each. By symmetry the domains meet at the midplane, so the interface sits in the bond region and β acts squarely on it. This is the cross-check for the β implementation: an independent Python calculation solves the same problem by a completely different route, and the two should agree.
Why the two are genuinely independent. The Python assembles the energy as a quadratic form on a cylindrical grid and solves a generalised eigenproblem directly, with free-space Poisson by zero-padded FFT. This solver relaxes in imaginary time on a Cartesian GPU grid with a multigrid Poisson and a moving free boundary. Nothing is shared but the physics, so agreement is evidence and disagreement locates a bug.
Reference values from the Python (R = 2.0, h = 0.05, E(H) = −0.501227):
β=0: −1.23264   0.1: −1.22987   0.2: −1.22249   0.4: −1.19843   0.8: −1.13374   1.146: −1.07585   2.0: −0.95693
Note those carry a standing puzzle: at β=0 the Python gives Ebind = 0.230 Ha where this framework is recorded at about 0.17. Whether that gap is the Python, the solver or the grid is exactly what this page should settle — and it must be settled before any β result is quoted.
β enters as an energy, not a condition. The surface term (β/2)∫ψ2dS has ∂nψ+βψ=0 as its natural boundary condition, so nothing is imposed. Set &curv=0 for the physical setting: β then supplies the boundary force by itself, which is the configuration validated by the concavity test.
