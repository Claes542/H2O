Sliding on a ring, with the partition specified
ne electrons over ni ions, the electron domains placed by hand as angular sectors and rotated rigidly by phi. Only the wavefunctions relax.
Why this rather than the free-boundary version. The same experiment run with a free boundary fails its own control: rotating by a full period must return the starting energy, and it missed by 370 meV, with every displaced configuration landing above the origin. That is the free boundary settling into a worse stuck point when started displaced — the failure this work documents across four geometries. Specifying the partition removes the search, so the control becomes exact by construction rather than something to hope for.
What sliding would look like. Not a small barrier — a flat E(phi). A charge-density wave in its sliding phase has a degenerate valley along the translation coordinate, a phason mode, and translating costs nothing. A pinned wave has a corrugated E(phi) with a finite Peierls–Nabarro amplitude. The signature is qualitative and hard to fake.
Two controls, both free. E(phi) must have period 1/(ni) in electron spacings, so phi = 1/ni must reproduce phi = 0 exactly — and with the partition specified it must do so to every digit, since the two configurations are related by a relabelling. And E(phi) must be symmetric about the half period. Any departure is numerical error, and bounds what pinning can be resolved.
